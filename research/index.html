Research

Research in representation theory and higher categorical structures

My research is in representation theory at the boundary where classical module theory meets the categorical world. I study how structural theorems for finite-dimensional algebras (Gabriel's Theorem foremost among them) can be lifted into tensor categories, using quivers as the bridge between combinatorics and abstract symmetry.

Research themes

Gabriel's Theorem in tensor categories

Gabriel's Theorem identifies representation-finite algebras with Dynkin quivers. The central question of the dissertation is what this theorem becomes when the ambient category of vector spaces is replaced by a tensor category: what should the quiver of an algebra object be, and which finiteness phenomena survive the passage.

Dissertation

Representation theory of Taft algebras

The Taft algebras are a family of pointed Hopf algebras whose representation categories are governed by cyclic quivers. This project develops their representation theory systematically — indecomposables, extensions, and the combinatorics of their module categories — as the primary test case for the categorical generalization.

Active

Module categories and Morita theory

Module categories over Rep(Taft) are classified via cyclic-quiver data. The Morita-theoretic side studies when two algebra objects in a tensor category have equivalent module categories, translating classical Morita equivalences into the equivariant and bimodule-categorical setting.

Active

Hopf algebras and their combinatorics

Pointed Hopf algebras, including the Taft algebras and their generalizations, have rich combinatorial structure encoded in group data and cocycles. This thread studies how that structure controls the representation theory and feeds into the quiver-theoretic classification results.

The organizing tool throughout is the quiver: a combinatorial object that encodes the structure of a module category in a form precise enough to classify, and concrete enough to compute with.
