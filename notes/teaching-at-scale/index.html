Consistency at Scale

Notes from four semesters inside a twenty-section coordinated course, and what it taught me about fairness as an engineering problem.

Teaching College Algebra in a coordinated program is, in part, a logistics problem. Twenty sections, twenty instructors, one shared promise to students: that the grade you earn does not depend on which room you happened to be assigned.

Holding that promise is harder than it sounds, and most of the work is invisible: calibrating rubrics, aligning pacing, comparing exam statistics across sections and asking why one cohort lagged.

Fairness is built, not assumed

The thing I did not expect is how much this resembles good mathematics: you fix the definitions first, agree on what counts as a correct argument, and only then start grading. Ambiguity in the rubric is exactly ambiguity in a theorem statement.
