Categorified Polynomials and the Cauchy Product

When an endofunctor replaces a linear operator, the scalar coefficients of a polynomial must also be replaced. The right replacement is a vector space for each degree. Addition becomes direct sum and multiplication becomes tensor product.

Given a linear operator $T : V \to V$, ordinary linear algebra studies expressions of the form

$$p(T) = a_0 \operatorname{id} + a_1 T + \cdots + a_n T^n, \qquad a_i \in k.$$

These are polynomials in $T$ with scalar coefficients. The minimal polynomial is the lowest-degree $p$ such that $p(T) = 0$. For a categorified endofunctor $S$, the powers $S^i$ still make sense. But the scalar coefficient $a_i \in k$ does not: there is no canonical way to attach a scalar to a functor.

The replacement is a vector space.

Categorified polynomial expressions

Definition. A categorified polynomial expression in $S$ is a functor of the form

$$P(S) = \bigoplus_{i=0}^n V_i \otimes S^i,$$

where each $V_i$ is a finite-dimensional $k$-vector space and $V_i \otimes S^i$ denotes the functor sending $M$ to $V_i \otimes_k S^i(M)$.

The direct sum is taken in the functor category $\operatorname{Fun}_k(\mathcal{A}, \mathcal{A})$.

Example. Take $k = \mathbb{R}$, $V_0 = \mathbb{R}^2$, $V_1 = \mathbb{R}$, and $V_i = 0$ for $i \geq 2$. The categorified polynomial $P(S) = \mathbb{R}^2 \otimes \operatorname{id} \oplus \mathbb{R} \otimes S$ sends $M$ to $M \oplus M \oplus S(M)$. The scalar shadow (defined below) is the polynomial $2 + x$.

The scalar shadow

Taking dimensions of the coefficient spaces gives the scalar shadow:

$$|P|(x) = \sum_{i=0}^n \dim_k(V_i) \cdot x^i \in k[x].$$

The categorified polynomial remembers more than its scalar shadow: it remembers the coefficient spaces themselves, not just their dimensions. A map of coefficient spaces $V_i \to W_i$ determines a natural transformation $P(S) \Rightarrow Q(S)$, but a map of polynomials $|P|(x) \to |Q|(x)$ is not a natural transformation.

Multiplication: the categorified Cauchy product

Ordinary polynomial multiplication uses the Cauchy product:

$$\left(\sum_i a_i x^i\right)\left(\sum_j b_j x^j\right) = \sum_n \left(\sum_{i+j=n} a_i b_j\right) x^n.$$

In the categorified version, scalar multiplication $a_i b_j$ becomes tensor product $V_i \otimes W_j$, and scalar addition $\sum_{i+j=n}$ becomes direct sum $\bigoplus_{i+j=n}$.

Theorem. For categorified polynomial expressions $P(S) = \bigoplus_i V_i \otimes S^i$ and $Q(S) = \bigoplus_j W_j \otimes S^j$, the composition

$$P(S) \circ Q(S) \cong \bigoplus_{n \geq 0} \left(\bigoplus_{i+j=n} V_i \otimes_k W_j\right) \otimes S^n.$$

Proof. Using $S^i \circ S^j = S^{i+j}$ and the distributivity of composition over finite direct sums in a $k$-linear category,

$$P(S) \circ Q(S) = \left(\bigoplus_i V_i \otimes S^i\right) \circ \left(\bigoplus_j W_j \otimes S^j\right) \cong \bigoplus_{i,j} (V_i \otimes S^i) \circ (W_j \otimes S^j) \cong \bigoplus_{i,j} V_i \otimes W_j \otimes S^{i+j},$$

and collecting terms by $n = i + j$ gives the stated formula. $\square$

Corollary. The scalar shadow satisfies $|P \circ Q|(x) = |P|(x) \cdot |Q|(x)$.

The corollary follows by taking dimensions.

What the Cauchy product means

The coefficient of $x^n$ in the classical product is $\sum_{i+j=n} a_i b_j$, a single number. The coefficient of $S^n$ in the categorified product is $\bigoplus_{i+j=n} V_i \otimes W_j$, a vector space.

The polynomial semiring $(\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 0}[x], +, \cdot)$ has been lifted to the semiring $(\text{finite-dimensional }k\text{-vector spaces over }k[x], \oplus, \otimes)$. Addition becomes direct sum and multiplication becomes tensor product. This is a concrete instance of the general principle: categorification replaces equalities by structure maps and numerical operations by their categorical counterparts.
