What a Reading Group Is For

Three years of organizing graduate reading groups on Hopf algebras, Lie theory, and tensor categories: a quiet case for slow, collective study.

The best mathematics I have learned, I learned in a room with six other people and a single hard book open between us. Reading groups are inefficient by design, and that is the point: you cannot skim past the thing you do not understand when someone is about to ask you to explain it.

Over three years I organized groups on Hopf algebras, on Lie algebras and their representations, and on tensor categories. Each followed the same arc: bravado in chapter one, humility by chapter three, fluency somewhere near the end if we were lucky.

A theorem you can state but not motivate is a theorem you have memorized, not learned.

I am increasingly convinced that motivation is the scarcest resource in graduate mathematics, and that talking through a proof with peers is the cheapest way to manufacture it.
