Orbit Towers and Iterated Eigenmaps

In linear algebra the Krylov sequence is a list of vectors in one space. Categorically it becomes a tower of maps across different objects. The difference is not cosmetic.

Given a linear operator $T : V \to V$ and a vector $v \in V$, one forms the Krylov sequence

$$v,\quad Tv,\quad T^2v,\quad T^3v,\quad \ldots$$

All of these live in $V$. The sequence is the raw material for cyclic subspaces, minimal polynomials, and Jordan theory.

For an endofunctor $S : \mathcal{A} \to \mathcal{A}$, the analogous construction is not a sequence of objects inside one ambient category (they already all live in $\mathcal{A}$) but a sequence of maps connecting successive iterates. The maps are the content; the objects alone do not determine them.

The orbit tower of a representation

Let $(M, s_M) \in \operatorname{Rep}(S)$. The structure map $s_M : M \to S(M)$ can be promoted to a map $S(s_M) : S(M) \to S^2(M)$ by applying $S$. Applying $S$ again gives $S^2(s_M) : S^2(M) \to S^3(M)$, and so on. Composing in sequence:

$$M \xrightarrow{s_M} S(M) \xrightarrow{S(s_M)} S^2(M) \xrightarrow{S^2(s_M)} S^3(M) \to \cdots$$

Definition. The orbit tower of $(M, s_M)$ is this sequence together with its transition maps.

The composite map from $M$ to $S^n(M)$ is

$$s_M^{(n)} = S^{n-1}(s_M) \circ S^{n-2}(s_M) \circ \cdots \circ S(s_M) \circ s_M,$$

with $s_M^{(0)} = \operatorname{id}_M$ by convention.

Lemma. For all $m, n \geq 0$,

$$s_M^{(m+n)} = S^m(s_M^{(n)}) \circ s_M^{(m)}.$$

Proof. By induction on $n$. The base case $n = 0$ gives $s_M^{(m)} = S^m(\operatorname{id}_M) \circ s_M^{(m)} = s_M^{(m)}$, which holds. For the inductive step, assume the formula holds for $n$. Then

$$s_M^{(m+n+1)} = S^{m+n}(s_M) \circ s_M^{(m+n)} = S^{m+n}(s_M) \circ S^m(s_M^{(n)}) \circ s_M^{(m)} = S^m(S^n(s_M) \circ s_M^{(n)}) \circ s_M^{(m)} = S^m(s_M^{(n+1)}) \circ s_M^{(m)},$$

where the third equality uses functoriality of $S^m$. $\square$

The difference from a Krylov sequence

In linear algebra, the Krylov sequence $v, Tv, T^2 v, \ldots$ is a sequence of elements of $V$. One can add them, scale them, compare them. The minimal polynomial of $v$ with respect to $T$ is the monic polynomial of least degree such that $p(T)v = 0$. All of this arithmetic happens inside one fixed vector space.

The orbit tower $M \to S(M) \to S^2(M) \to \cdots$ is a sequence of morphisms between different objects. The objects $S^n(M)$ are not necessarily comparable or addable. The structure lies entirely in the maps.

This is not a deficiency. It is a feature: the maps record how $M$ travels through the iterates of $S$, and that record carries more information than the bare sequence of objects.

Orbit towers for canonical eigenobjects

When $(M, s_M) = (M, \lambda_M)$ is a canonical $\lambda$-eigenobject, the orbit tower takes a particularly uniform form.

Proposition. For a canonical $\lambda$-eigenobject $(M, \lambda_M)$, the transition map from $S^n(M)$ to $S^{n+1}(M)$ in the orbit tower is $S^n(\lambda_M) = \lambda_{S^n(M)}$.

Proof. The map $S^n(\lambda_M)$ is the component of the natural transformation $\lambda$ applied to $S^n(M)$, which is exactly $\lambda_{S^n(M)}$ by naturality. $\square$

So the orbit tower of a canonical eigenobject is:

$$M \xrightarrow{\lambda_M} S(M) \xrightarrow{\lambda_{S(M)}} S^2(M) \xrightarrow{\lambda_{S^2(M)}} S^3(M) \to \cdots$$

Every transition map is a component of the same natural transformation $\lambda$. This is the categorical analogue of the sequence

$$v,\quad \mu v,\quad \mu^2 v,\quad \mu^3 v,\quad \ldots$$

where every step multiplies by the same scalar $\mu$. But the powers are not scalar powers: they are iterated natural maps through successive powers of $S$.

What comes next

The orbit tower is the raw sequence. What makes classical linear algebra powerful is that this sequence eventually becomes dependent: a polynomial relation $p(T)v = 0$ holds. The categorical analogue requires first understanding how two endofunctors can commute, and what it means for one to preserve the eigenstructure of the other. That is the subject of the next post.
