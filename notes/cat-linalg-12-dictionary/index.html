The Complete Dictionary

A summary of the full series: every concept from elementary linear algebra alongside its categorical replacement, and the organizing principle that connects them.

This is the capstone post of the series. It does not prove new results. It collects the complete translation between linear algebra and its categorification, states the series thesis precisely, and notes what the categorical version adds beyond the classical one.

What the categorified version adds

The dictionary above might suggest that categorification is a straightforward substitution. It is not. Three features of the categorical theory have no classical counterpart.

Structure versus condition. In linear algebra, the eigenvalue equation $Tv = \mu v$ is a condition on $v$. A vector either satisfies it or does not. In the categorical theory, the structure map $s_M : M \to S(M)$ is data attached to $M$. Every object of $\mathcal{A}$ is an eigenobject once a map is chosen; the question is which map is chosen.

Isomorphism class versus equality. The commutation condition $ST = TS$ in linear algebra is an equality. The categorical version is a natural isomorphism $\alpha : ST \xRightarrow{\sim} TS$, which is extra data, not a property. Two different natural isomorphisms $\alpha$ and $\alpha'$ give two different commutation data and two different lifted functors.

Scalar shadow versus internal structure. The scalar shadow of a categorified polynomial remembers only dimensions. The polynomial itself remembers the coefficient spaces and the isomorphisms between them. A factorization of the scalar shadow does not determine a factorization datum; the factorization datum carries strictly more information.

The series thesis

The series began with one question:

What remains of elementary linear algebra if a vector space is replaced by a $k$-linear category and a linear operator is replaced by an endofunctor?

The answer, in one sentence:

The familiar path from eigenvectors to minimal polynomials survives categorification, but only after every equality is replaced by coherent structure and every scalar coefficient is replaced by a space of coefficients.

Categorification does not copy equations literally. It replaces equalities by structure maps, conditions by data, and compatibility equations by naturality diagrams. The classical theory is the shadow cast when all that structure is compressed into numbers by taking dimensions and passing to $K_0$.
