The Same Compatibility Equation on Both Sides

The object-side preservation theorem produced one compatibility equation. Transposing everything and asking the same question on functionals produces the same equation, written on the other side of α. This is not a coincidence.

Post IV established that the lifted functor $\widetilde{T}$ preserves canonical $\lambda$-eigenobjects if and only if

$$\alpha_M^{-1} \circ T(\lambda_M) = \lambda_{T(M)}$$

for every object $M$. This is the central compatibility equation of the object side.

Now transpose everything. The commutation datum $\alpha : ST \Rightarrow TS$ transposes to a natural isomorphism $\alpha^* : T^* S^* \Rightarrow S^* T^*$. The lifted functor $T^*$ acts on $\mathcal{A}^*$, and the question is: when does $T^*$ preserve canonical $\lambda^*$-eigenfunctionals?

The transposed commutation datum

Given $\alpha : ST \xRightarrow{\sim} TS$, define the transposed commutation datum.

Definition. For functionals $F \in \mathcal{A}^*$, the component of $\alpha^*$ at $F$ is the natural transformation

$$\alpha^*_F = F(\alpha) : F \circ S \circ T \Rightarrow F \circ T \circ S,$$

where $F(\alpha)$ denotes postcomposition with the components of $\alpha$.

More precisely, at an object $M$, the component is $F(\alpha_M) : F(S(T(M))) \to F(T(S(M)))$.

The functional-side preservation theorem

Theorem. The lifted functor $T^* : \operatorname{Rep}(S^*) \to \operatorname{Rep}(S^*)$ preserves canonical $\lambda^*$-eigenfunctionals if and only if

$$T(\lambda_M) = \alpha_M \circ \lambda_{T(M)}$$

for every object $M \in \mathcal{A}$.

Proof. A canonical $\lambda^*$-eigenfunctional is a pair $(F, F(\lambda))$. The functor $T^*$ acts by $T^*(F) = F \circ T$. Its structure map in $\operatorname{Rep}(S^*)$ is $\alpha^*_F \circ T^*(F(\lambda))$, which at an object $M$ is the composite

$$(F \circ T)(M) \xrightarrow{F(T(\lambda_M))} (F \circ T \circ S)(M) \xrightarrow{F(\alpha_M)^{-1}} (F \circ S \circ T)(M).$$

Wait: for preservation of $\operatorname{Eig}_{\lambda^*}(S^*)$, the structure map of $T^*(F, F(\lambda))$ must equal $(F \circ T)(\lambda)$, which at $M$ is $F(\lambda_{T(M)})$. So the condition is

$$F(\alpha_M)^{-1} \circ F(T(\lambda_M)) = F(\lambda_{T(M)})$$

for all $F$ and all $M$. Applying this to the representable functionals $\operatorname{Hom}(-, N)$ and using the Yoneda lemma, the condition reduces to

$$\alpha_M^{-1} \circ T(\lambda_M) = \lambda_{T(M)},$$

which is the object-side equation, or equivalently $T(\lambda_M) = \alpha_M \circ \lambda_{T(M)}$. $\square$

Reading the two equations together

Object side:

$$\alpha_M^{-1} \circ T(\lambda_M) = \lambda_{T(M)}.$$

Functional side:

$$T(\lambda_M) = \alpha_M \circ \lambda_{T(M)}.$$

These are the same equation. Multiply both sides of the object-side equation by $\alpha_M$ on the left to obtain the functional-side equation. The two conditions are equivalent, and each implies the other.

The transpose story does not produce a new compatibility law. It reveals that the original one was already self-dual: it reads the same from both sides of $\alpha_M$.

What this means structurally

In classical linear algebra, the eigenvalue equation $Tv = \mu v$ is symmetric in a similar way: the same scalar $\mu$ appears whether one works on $V$ or on $V^*$. The categorified version makes this symmetry explicit through the equation above.

The compatibility equation is not merely a condition that appears on one side of the theory. It is the organizing equation of the whole structure: it governs preservation of eigendata on objects, preservation of eigendata on functionals, and (as the next posts will show) the behavior of polynomial expressions in $T$.

The third and final part of the series turns to polynomial calculus.
