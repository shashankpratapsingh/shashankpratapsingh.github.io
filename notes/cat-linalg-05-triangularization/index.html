Triangularization and Diagonalization Without Matrices

Classical triangularization puts operators into upper-triangular form. The categorical version has nothing to do with matrices. It is about preserving a filtration, and the matrices are what you see when you forget most of the structure.

Simultaneous triangularization in linear algebra says that a commuting family of operators can sometimes be put into upper-triangular form with respect to a common basis. But the basis is not the point. The basis is a way of representing a flag: a chain of invariant subspaces

$$0 = V_0 \subsetneq V_1 \subsetneq \cdots \subsetneq V_n = V,$$

each stable under every operator in the family. The matrix is upper-triangular because the flag is invariant; the flag is the real structure.

Categorically, flags of subspaces become filtrations by full subcategories, and the matrices disappear entirely.

Categorical filtrations

Definition. A filtration of $\mathcal{A}$ is a chain of full additive subcategories

$$0 = \mathcal{A}_0 \subseteq \mathcal{A}_1 \subseteq \cdots \subseteq \mathcal{A}_n = \mathcal{A}.$$

Here $0$ denotes the zero subcategory containing only the zero object.

A family of endofunctors $\{T_i\}_{i \in I}$ preserves the filtration if each $T_i$ maps $\mathcal{A}_r$ into $\mathcal{A}_r$ for every $r$.

Definition. The family is simultaneously triangularizable along the filtration if each functor $T_i$ preserves the filtration and the induced functor on each successive quotient $\mathcal{A}_r / \mathcal{A}_{r-1}$ carries canonical eigendata.

The quotient $\mathcal{A}_r / \mathcal{A}_{r-1}$ is the categorical analogue of the diagonal block in a triangular matrix.

Simultaneous diagonalization

In linear algebra, simultaneous diagonalization is stronger than triangularization: the common invariant flag splits.

Definition. The family $\{T_i\}$ is simultaneously diagonalizable if there is a decomposition

$$\mathcal{A} \simeq \bigoplus_{\beta \in B} \mathcal{A}_\beta$$

such that each $\mathcal{A}_\beta$ is stable under every $T_i$, and each $T_i$ acts on $\mathcal{A}_\beta$ through a canonical eigenvalue $\lambda_\beta^{(i)} : \operatorname{id} \Rightarrow T_i|_{\mathcal{A}_\beta}$.

The index set $B$ is the categorical analogue of the set of simultaneous eigenvalues.

Proposition. If $\{T_i\}$ preserves a filtration and the induced action on each successive quotient is through canonical eigendata for all $i$ simultaneously, then the family is simultaneously triangularizable along that filtration.

Proof. The condition in the proposition is exactly the definition of simultaneous triangularizability. $\square$

The content is in the converse direction and in the sufficient conditions, which depend on the ambient category. For abelian categories with enough structure, analogues of the classical results hold; the proof strategy follows the classical one with subspaces replaced by subcategories and scalar conditions replaced by naturality conditions.

What the matrices were hiding

Upper-triangular matrices have zeros below the diagonal. Those zeros encode the fact that the flag is preserved: the functor does not move objects down the filtration. The off-diagonal entries encode how objects in $\mathcal{A}_r$ map into $\mathcal{A}_{r+1}$ after reduction.

Categorically, both pieces of information are already in the functor and the filtration. No matrix coordinates are needed to state them. The matrix representation is a shadow that appears once you choose bases for the successive quotients; it is not the underlying structure.

The takeaway

Triangularization is not about matrices. It is about preserving a filtration. Diagonalization is not about diagonal matrices. It is about splitting into stable eigenblocks.

The categorical formulation makes this visible by stripping away the basis-dependent language. What remains is a structural property of the functor and the category, stated in terms that make no reference to coordinates.
