Double Duals and Eigenfunctionals

Reflexivity is automatic for finite-dimensional vector spaces. For k-linear categories it is a genuine condition. Meanwhile the eigenvalue datum on the object side induces eigenstructure on every functional, automatically and without extra choices.

The previous post defined the canonical functor $J_{\mathcal{A}} : \mathcal{A} \to \mathcal{A}^{**}$ by $J_{\mathcal{A}}(M)(F) = F(M)$. For a finite-dimensional vector space $V$, the analogous map $V \to V^{**}$ is an isomorphism. The categorical version need not be an equivalence, and the failure of reflexivity is a meaningful geometric and algebraic datum, not a defect of the definition.

Reflexivity as a condition

Definition. A $k$-linear category $\mathcal{A}$ is reflexive if $J_{\mathcal{A}} : \mathcal{A} \to \mathcal{A}^{**}$ is an equivalence of categories.

For $\mathcal{A} = \operatorname{Vect}_k^{\text{fd}}$, the category of finite-dimensional $k$-vector spaces, the functor $J_{\mathcal{A}}$ is an equivalence. This recovers the classical fact.

For $\mathcal{A} = \operatorname{Mod}(A)$, the category of all modules over a $k$-algebra $A$, the functor $J_{\mathcal{A}}$ is generally not an equivalence. The failure encodes information about the size and structure of $A$.

The key point is that in the categorical setting, reflexivity must be checked. The classical proof for finite-dimensional spaces relies on dimension counts that have no direct categorical counterpart.

Eigenfunctionals

The eigenvalue datum $\lambda : \operatorname{id}_{\mathcal{A}} \Rightarrow S$ induces eigenstructure on every functional automatically.

Definition. The functional-side representation category $\operatorname{Rep}(S^*)$ has as objects pairs $(F, \sigma_F)$ where $F : \mathcal{A} \to \operatorname{Vect}_k$ is a $k$-linear functor and $\sigma_F : F \Rightarrow F \circ S$ is a natural transformation.

A morphism $\eta : (F, \sigma_F) \to (G, \sigma_G)$ in $\operatorname{Rep}(S^*)$ is a natural transformation $\eta : F \Rightarrow G$ satisfying $\sigma_G \circ \eta = ({\eta \circ S}) \circ \sigma_F$, where $\eta \circ S$ denotes the whiskering.

Given the natural transformation $\lambda : \operatorname{id} \Rightarrow S$ and any functional $F \in \mathcal{A}^*$, define

$$F(\lambda) : F \Rightarrow F \circ S$$

by $(F(\lambda))_M = F(\lambda_M) : F(M) \to F(S(M))$.

Proposition. The natural transformation $F(\lambda)$ is well-defined: it is natural in $M$.

Proof. For a morphism $f : M \to N$, functoriality of $F$ gives $F(S(f)) \circ F(\lambda_M) = F(S(f) \circ \lambda_M)$. Naturality of $\lambda$ gives $S(f) \circ \lambda_M = \lambda_N \circ f$, so $F(S(f)) \circ F(\lambda_M) = F(\lambda_N \circ f) = F(\lambda_N) \circ F(f)$. This is the naturality condition for $F(\lambda)$. $\square$

Definition. The canonical $\lambda$-eigenfunctional associated to $F$ is the pair $(F, F(\lambda))$.

The category $\operatorname{Eig}_{\lambda^*}(S^*)$ consists of all canonical $\lambda$-eigenfunctionals, with morphisms those natural transformations $\eta : F \Rightarrow G$ satisfying the functorial compatibility with $F(\lambda)$ and $G(\lambda)$.

The eigenvalue datum induces a natural transformation on $\mathcal{A}^*$

Theorem. The assignment $F \mapsto F(\lambda)$ defines a natural transformation

$$\lambda^* : \operatorname{id}_{\mathcal{A}^*} \Rightarrow S^*.$$

Proof. The component of $\lambda^*$ at $F$ is $F(\lambda) : F \Rightarrow F \circ S = S^*(F)$. Naturality of $\lambda^*$ requires: for a natural transformation $\eta : F \Rightarrow G$, the square

$$(\eta \circ S) \circ F(\lambda) = G(\lambda) \circ \eta$$

commutes. At an object $M$, this is $\eta_{S(M)} \circ F(\lambda_M) = G(\lambda_M) \circ \eta_M$, which holds by naturality of $\eta$ applied to the morphism $\lambda_M : M \to S(M)$. $\square$

The structure of the result

The object side: $\lambda : \operatorname{id}_{\mathcal{A}} \Rightarrow S$ is given. The functional side: $\lambda^* : \operatorname{id}_{\mathcal{A}^*} \Rightarrow S^*$ is derived from it.

No choices are made on the functional side. The eigenvalue datum on objects induces, canonically, an eigenvalue datum on functionals. This is the functional analogue of the fact that a scalar $\mu$ determines its action on the dual space as well as on the original space.
