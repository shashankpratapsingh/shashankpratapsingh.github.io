From Operators to Endofunctors

The equation Tv = μv cannot be written in a category. This post explains what replaces it, and why the replacement is richer than the original.

A linear operator $T : V \to V$ acts on vectors inside one fixed ambient space. The equation $Tv = \mu v$ makes sense because both sides live in $V$. Once that ambient space is replaced by a category, this sentence stops parsing. The left side $S(M)$ is an object produced by a functor; the right side $\mu \cdot M$ has no meaning without scalar multiplication on objects. The right response is not to force the equation to make sense. It is to find the structure that the equation was secretly encoding.

What a linear operator actually does

The equation $Tv = \mu v$ records two things at once: that $T$ acts, and that the result is canonically related back to the input by the scalar $\mu$. The scalar is doing real work. It provides a specific map $v \mapsto \mu v$ that compares $Tv$ with $v$.

In a category, there is no canonical map from $M$ to $S(M)$ built into the definition of $S$. The functor $S$ moves objects forward; it does not automatically look back. So the analogue of an eigenvalue equation must supply that backward comparison explicitly.

The ambient setting

Throughout this series, $\mathcal{A}$ denotes a $k$-linear category: a category in which every hom-set $\operatorname{Hom}_{\mathcal{A}}(M, N)$ is a $k$-vector space and composition is $k$-bilinear. The endofunctor $S : \mathcal{A} \to \mathcal{A}$ is assumed $k$-linear, meaning the induced maps on hom-sets are $k$-linear.

The category $\operatorname{Mod}(A)$ of modules over a $k$-algebra $A$, with a fixed endofunctor such as tensoring with a bimodule, is the canonical example to keep in mind.

The category Rep(S)

The replacement for the eigenvalue equation is not an equation at all. It is a structured object.

Definition. A representation of $S$ is a pair $(M, s_M)$ where $M$ is an object of $\mathcal{A}$ and $s_M : M \to S(M)$ is a morphism in $\mathcal{A}$.

A morphism $f : (M, s_M) \to (N, s_N)$ in $\operatorname{Rep}(S)$ is a morphism $f : M \to N$ in $\mathcal{A}$ satisfying

$$S(f) \circ s_M = s_N \circ f.$$

This commutativity condition says that the structure map is preserved: applying $S$ to $f$ and then using $s_N$ gives the same result as first using $s_M$ and then applying $S(f)$. Visually:

$$\begin{array}{ccc} M & \xrightarrow{\,s_M\,} & S(M) \\ \downarrow\!f & & \downarrow\!S(f) \\ N & \xrightarrow{\,s_N\,} & S(N) \end{array}$$

Proposition. The data above defines a category $\operatorname{Rep}(S)$.

Proof. We check identity morphisms and composition.

For the identity on $(M, s_M)$: the morphism $\operatorname{id}_M : M \to M$ satisfies

$$S(\operatorname{id}_M) \circ s_M = \operatorname{id}_{S(M)} \circ s_M = s_M = s_M \circ \operatorname{id}_M,$$

using $k$-linearity of $S$ and the unit axiom for identities.

For composition: suppose $f : (M, s_M) \to (N, s_N)$ and $g : (N, s_N) \to (P, s_P)$ are morphisms. Then

$$S(g \circ f) \circ s_M = S(g) \circ S(f) \circ s_M = S(g) \circ s_N \circ f = s_P \circ g \circ f,$$

where the first equality uses functoriality of $S$, the second uses the morphism condition for $f$, and the third uses the morphism condition for $g$. So $g \circ f$ is a morphism $(M, s_M) \to (P, s_P)$. $\square$

What the structure map is

The structure map $s_M : M \to S(M)$ is the categorical replacement for the scalar $\mu$ in the equation $Tv = \mu v$.

In linear algebra, the scalar $\mu$ attaches to every vector $v$ a specific element $\mu v$ of the same space, coherently as $v$ varies. In the categorical setting, the structure map $s_M$ attaches to the object $M$ a specific morphism into $S(M)$, coherently as $M$ varies through morphisms.

An object $(M, s_M) \in \operatorname{Rep}(S)$ is therefore not just an object on which $S$ acts. It is an object equipped with a chosen comparison between where it starts and where $S$ sends it.

The first dictionary entry

The translation table between linear algebra and $\operatorname{Rep}(S)$ begins here:

Linear algebra Categorified version
vector space $V$ $k$-linear category $\mathcal{A}$
linear operator $T : V \to V$ endofunctor $S : \mathcal{A} \to \mathcal{A}$
vector $v \in V$ object $M \in \mathcal{A}$
image $Tv$ image $S(M)$
eigenvalue equation $Tv = \mu v$ structure map $s_M : M \to S(M)$
eigenspace $E_\mu(T)$ category $\operatorname{Rep}(S)$

The next post refines the right column: not every structure map is the categorical analogue of an eigenvalue equation. The ones that are require the map $s_M$ to be canonical, meaning determined by a single natural transformation rather than chosen independently for each object. That is the correct categorification of the scalar $\mu$.
